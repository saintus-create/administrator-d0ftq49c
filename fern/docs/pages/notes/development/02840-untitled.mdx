There is no unified national dataset of college-related deaths\n\nMultiple sources explicitly note that there is **no central system** tracking all college-related deaths by year, cause, and activity:\n\n* For hazing, researchers emphasize that **no U.S. health agency tracks hazing incidents, injuries, or deaths**; data are pieced together from media, court records, and volunteers. [[1]] The Wikipedia list of hazing deaths likewise notes that there is **no central system for tracking hazing deaths** and that inclusion is based on case-by-case judgments. [[2]]\n* The new hazing deaths database (HazingInfo.org) was only launched recently to stitch together 1838�present cases; even now, it is built from historical records and journalism, not official mortality coding. [[3]]\n* On study-abroad deaths, an AP investigation reported that when a parent tried to find national data, �**nobody was keeping track of this at all**�; U.S. colleges must report on-campus deaths, but **they are not required to disclose most student deaths abroad**, and federal agencies do not maintain a comprehensive count. [[10]] [[11]]\n- NIAAA�s current �College Drinking� materials summarize this as roughly **1,519 alcohol-related unintentional-injury deaths per year** among college students aged 18�24. [[12]] [[13]] \n\nThese are **large national counts of non-natural deaths**, but:\n\n- They cover only **one cause category** (alcohol-related unintentional injuries).\n- They **mix all contexts** (on- and off-campus, travel, parties, etc.).\n- They are **not broken out by �college activity�** and are only reported for selected years (1998, 2001, 2005, with later trend work to ~2014) [[14]]�not as an annual 2000�2026 series.\n\n---\n\n## 3. [[10]] [[12]]\n   - Clery covers only crimes in **campus geography** (murder, manslaughter, etc.), not suicides, overdoses, or most accidents. [[24]] [[25]]\n   - NCCSIR covers only **school-sponsored sports**. [[26]]\n   - HazingInfo captures **known hazing deaths**, but those are just one subset of college activities. [[15]] \n\n2. **No dataset systematically encodes �college activity� at time of death.**  \n   Turner�s mortality study, NIAAA analyses, and NCCSIR mostly label **cause (suicide, traffic accident, heat stroke, etc.) and context (in sport, alcohol-related)**�not �fraternity initiation,� �field trip,� �laboratory accident,� etc. [[7]] [[18]] Activity labels have to be inferred from narrative case reports, which is not done nationwide.\n\n3. **Time coverage and granularity are inconsistent.**  \n   - Comprehensive mortality studies exist for **one or several years**, not continuously 2000�2026. [[10]] [[14]]\n   - HazingInfo gives **overall since-2000 counts and peaks** but does not (in public summaries) publish a full year-by-year table of *college-only* hazing deaths with cause and activity. [[27]] [[3]]\n   - The AP and related outlets emphasize that **study-abroad deaths are not systematically tracked** by U.S. agencies or many institutions. [[4]] [[5]]\n   - Higher-ed analysts point out that even **overall cause-of-death statistics for college students aren�t routinely produced**; the Turner study was a one-off pilot. [[6]]\n\n---\n\n## 5.
