Commentary
Pen. Code, � 236.14�allows a petitioner who was a victim of human trafficking, to petition the court for an order vacating his/her arrest and conviction of a nonviolent offense that was the direct result of being a victim of human trafficking �which demonstrates that the person lacked the requisite intent to commit the offense. Upon this showing, the court shall find that the person lacked the requisite intent to commit the offense and shall therefore vacate the conviction as invalid due to legal defect at the time of the arrest or conviction.� (See�Pen. C. � 236.14(a)).
Pleading and Proof
Burden of Proof:�The Petitioner must establish by clear and convincing evidence that the arrest or conviction was the direct result of being a victim of human trafficking. The petition for relief shall be submitted under penalty of perjury and shall describe all of the available grounds and evidence that the petitioner was a victim of human trafficking, and the arrest or conviction of a nonviolent offense was the direct result of being a victim of human trafficking. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(a)(b)).
Rebuttable Presumption:�A petitioner under sectionʤ 236.14�is entitled to a rebuttable presumption that that the requirements for relief have been met.
No Opposition:�If opposition to the petition is not filed by the applicable state or local prosecutorial agency, the court shall deem the petition unopposed and may grant the petition. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(d)).
Hearing:�If a hearing is required, it may consist of testimony by the petitioner, evidence and supporting documentation in support of the petition, and evidence presented by any of the involved state or local prosecutorial agencies that obtained the conviction. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(f)). At the conclusion of the hearing, the court may vacate the arrest and conviction if it finds each of the following:
		(1) That the petitioner was a victim of human trafficking at the time the nonviolent crime was committed;
		(2) The commission of the crime was a direct result of being a victim of human trafficking;
		(3) The victim is engaged in a good faith effort to distance themselves from the human trafficking scheme; and
		(4) It is in the best interest of the petitioner and in the interests of justice to vacate the conviction and the arrest. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(g)).
Effect of Order:�The issuance of the order shall have the following effect:
		(1) A finding that the petitioner was a victim of human trafficking when they committed the offense;
		(2) The guilty verdict is set aside and the accusation or information against the petitioner is set aside; and
		(3) The Department of Justice is notified that the petitioner was a victim of human trafficking when they committed the crime and that the requested relief has been ordered. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(h)).
Sealing and Destruction of Records:�If the court issues an order pursuant to sections (a) and (j), the court shall also order all of the agencies enumerated in (k)(1) to seal and destroy their records.
Service and Filing:�A petition pursuant to this section shall be made and heard at any time after the person has ceased to be a victim of human trafficking, or at any time after the petitioner has sought services for being a victim of human trafficking, whichever occurs later. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(l)).
The petition for relief and supporting documentation shall be served on the state or local prosecutorial agency that obtained the conviction for which vacatur is sought or with jurisdiction over charging decisions with regard to the arrest. The state or local prosecutorial agency shall have 45 days from the date of receipt of service to respond to the petition for relief (Pen. Code, � 236.14(c)).
The court may, with the agreement of the petitioner and all of the involved state or local prosecutorial agencies, consolidate into one hearing a petition with multiple convictions from different jurisdictions. (Pen. Code, � 236.14(e)).
