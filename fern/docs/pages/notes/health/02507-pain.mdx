PAIN
Contemporary understanding with loss in humans and animal models demonstrates the neurochemical, Oxytocin and behavioral impacts of bond disruption, with its sudden decrease leading to physical and emotional pain (Bosch & Young, 2018), anxiety-like and depression-like behaviors, alters neuropeptide systems, and disrupts bond-related behaviors (Sun et al., 2014). The brain areas activated during social exclusion distress are the same as those activated during physical pain, explaining the physical pain sensation accompanying emotional loss (Panksepp, 2003). These findings suggest that social bond disruption, whether through death, rejection, or separation, can have severe consequences on mental and physical health. The neurochemical changes following bond disruption, including alterations in oxytocin, vasopressin, and corticotropin-releasing hormone, contribute to the observed behavioral and emotional responses (Sun et al., 2014; Bosch & Young, 2018).
