Definitions
For purposes of this Agreement, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:
Brady Violation 1.1 A Brady violation occurs when the prosecution fails to disclose material favorable evidence to the accused, in violation of the due process requirements established in Brady v. Maryland, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), and subsequent case law. This duty to disclose exists regardless of whether the accused has made a request for such evidence, and irrespective of the good faith or bad faith of the prosecution.
Favorable Evidence 1.2 Favorable evidence refers to any evidence that is either (a) exculpatory, meaning it tends to negate the accused's guilt, or (b) impeachment evidence, which could be used to undermine the credibility of a witness or the prosecution's case. Both exculpatory and impeachment evidence are treated equally under the Brady doctrine.
Exculpatory Evidence 1.3 Exculpatory evidence is any evidence that tends to show the accused's innocence or mitigate their culpability in the alleged offense.
Impeachment Evidence 1.4 Impeachment evidence includes any information that could be used to challenge the credibility, biases, prejudices, self-interests, or motives of a witness or the prosecution, such as evidence that a witness has a history of dishonesty or a motive to fabricate testimony.
Prosecutorial Misconduct 1.5 Prosecutorial misconduct refers to any actions or omissions by a prosecutor that violate their ethical or legal obligations, including but not limited to:
(a) Selective prosecution, where the accused is intentionally singled out for prosecution based on impermissible factors such as race, religion, or the exercise of constitutional rights;
(b) Vindictive prosecution, where the prosecution is initiated in retaliation for the accused's exercise of legal rights or to punish the accused for asserting their rights; and
(c) Suppression of evidence, including the failure to disclose favorable evidence as required under Brady.
Discursive Manipulation 1.6 Discursive manipulation refers to the strategic use of language and framing to shape narratives and influence perceptions, often with the intent of undermining legal rights or protections. Tactics may include selective emphasis and omissions, moral inversion (portraying the victim as the aggressor), procedural legitimization (lending an air of neutrality to biased actions), and the use of legal formalism to mask political motivations.
Anti-SLAPP Protections 1.7 Anti-SLAPP (Strategic Lawsuit Against Public Participation) protections refer to legal safeguards designed to prevent meritless lawsuits or legal actions that are intended to chill or retaliate against individuals for exercising their rights to free speech or public participation on matters of public concern.
