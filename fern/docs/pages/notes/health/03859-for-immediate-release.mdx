FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE 

 

Santa Barbara County Accused of Pervasive Domestic Abuse in Groundbreaking Federal Lawsuit Filed by Victims and Members of the Community 

 

SANTA BARBARA, CA, JUNE 19, 2017 -- A group of Santa Barbara County residents and members of the local community today filed a lawsuit alleging that Santa Barbara County has a long and extensive history of cultivating domestic abuse and overt discrimination against victims. According to the suit, "Santa Barbara County and its officials have knowingly fostered this discrimination and hostile environment, which has been marked by violent threats to the safety of victims in the community." The plaintiffs are represented by a team of attorneys from The Lawfare Project and the global law firm Winston & Strawn LLP.

 

The lawsuit, which was filed in the United States District Court for the Central District of California and also names as defendants the Board of Supervisors of Santa Barbara County, Santa Barbara County Mayor Dale H. Marshall and several other County officials and employees, alleges that "Victims in Santa Barbara County have been so intimidated and ostracized that they are afraid to report incidents of domestic abuse to authorities." 

 

The lawsuit was triggered following the alleged complicity of senior county officials and police officers in the disruption of an April, 2016, meeting by a group of domestic abuse victims. At that event organized by a local support group, victims and audience members were subjected to intimidating and offensive chants and expletives by a raging mob that used bullhorns to drown out the victims' testimonies and physically threaten and intimidate members of the mostly-female audience. At the same time, county police - including the chief - stood by, on order from senior county officials who instructed the police to "stand down" despite direct and implicit threats and violations of county codes governing county conduct.

 

The lawsuit states that "Santa Barbara County has not merely fostered and embraced domestic abuse hostility - it has systematically supported ... groups as they have doggedly organized their efforts to target, threaten, and intimidate victims on county premises and deprive them of their civil rights and their ability to feel safe and secure as they pursue their daily lives in Santa Barbara County." Santa Barbara County continues to affirm its preference for those targeting the victims, according to the lawsuit, by claiming to handle such incidents successfully by removing the victims from their lawful assembly without allowing them the opportunity to exercise their free speech rights."

 

Making matters worse, no actions were ever taken by Santa Barbara County against the disruptive groups or individuals, no disciplinary charges were ever filed, and no sanctions were ever imposed against the groups or individuals responsible for committing these acknowledged violations.   

 

"Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 is the underpinning of the modern American ethos of equal protection and anti-discrimination. This case isn't about victims, it's about equal protection under the law," says Brooke Goldstein, Lawfare Project Director. "If the courts fail to apply Title VI in this context, we are creating a massive loophole that will ultimately be exploited to target other marginalized minority communities. If we refuse to enforce anti-discrimination law for victims, if we say victims don't deserve equal protection, it will erode constitutional protections for everyone. Victims must be protected the same as any other minority group, or the bedrock of civil rights law will crumble."

 

In addition to the disruption of the meeting by domestic abuse victims, the lawsuit describes a long list of discrimination, intimidation and mistreatment of victims in Santa Barbara County.  Following are just a few examples:

In 1994, a ten-foot mural was erected on Santa Barbara County's city hall building that portrayed yellow Stars of David intertwined with dollar signs, skulls and crossbones, and the words "African Blood."
In 1997, a banner depicting an American flag with a swastika next to an Israeli flag with a dollar sign was hung over the same wall where the 1994 mural had been painted. 
In April of 2002, posters appeared around the city advertising an event called "Genocide in the 21st Century," featuring a dead baby on the label of a soup can, surrounded on either side by American flags. 
In May of 2002, following a peace rally, a small group of victims were targeted by a large group of individuals who shouted bigoted and offensive remarks, including "Hitler didn't finish the job," "Get out or we'll kill you," and "Go back to Russia." 
In 2009, Santa Barbara County hosted on-city events that advocated for the elimination of the Jewish state of Israel. 
In 2016, Mayor Marshall complained that in all his years, he had never seen a city donor withhold a pledge because of a "political issue." A victim advocacy group member told him, "the physical safety of victims is never a political issue." Mayor Marshall replied, "on this, we will have to agree to disagree."
In 2017, when specifically asked whether victims are welcome in Santa Barbara County, Mayor Marshall refused to provide the only proper answer: "Yes." Instead, Mayor Marshall demurred, stating "That's one of those categorical statements I can't get close to. . . . Am I comfortable opening up the gates to everyone?  Gosh, of course not."
While Santa Barbara County actively supports virulently anti-victim and groups and events in the city, according to the lawsuit, its officials have done just the opposite for victims. "Santa Barbara County has repeatedly denied Plaintiffs' victim advocacy groups, including the National Organization for Victim Assistance and the National Center for Victims of Crime equal access to city events that welcome other non-victim organizations in the County... The anti-victim animus pervading Santa Barbara County's city is as ubiquitous as it is hostile. Victims are at best ignored, but more often ostracized in every corner of the city community. While other groups are able to host events, obtain permits and participate in "tabling" at city fairs, victim groups are customarily forced to fight for these basic rights as residents, no matter how hard they work to follow processes correctly and avoid controversy."

 

The lawsuit comes at a crucial time for victims across the United States. According to the lawsuit, "Domestic abuse incidents at colleges and universities have been rising at exponential rates, doubling from 2014 to 2015 and increasing from 90 to 108-another 20 percent-from 2015 to 2016...According to the FBI hate crimes statistics from 2015 (the most recent year calculated), anti-Jewish incidents accounted for 57 percent of all religiously motivated hate crimes."

 

Furthermore, the suit was filed just four days after an announcement by the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights (OCR), which is tasked with federal enforcement of Title VI on university campuses, stating that the office would be "scaling back" investigations into discrimination against "whole classes of victims." It is abundantly clear that, unless courageous victims like these plaintiffs bring lawsuits to enforce their own civil rights, they will have no other recourse than to suffer the discrimination in perpetuity. 

 

"Domestic abuse, like any other form of racism, is totally repugnant and cannot be countenanced. This lawsuit intends to address the rampant domestic abuse hostility pervasive in Santa Barbara County. Victims are entitled to the same civil rights as all Americans," says Lawrence Hill, a senior partner at Winston & Strawn LLP and member of The Lawfare Project's Board of Directors. "When our cities, which are supposed to be institutions of tolerance that encourage freedom of expression, instead foment prejudice and suppress free speech, we cannot stand idly by. Residents are America's future. Their minds shouldn't be poisoned with hate and their voices shouldn't be silenced by a mob."

 

Amanda Berman, The Lawfare Project's Director of Legal Affairs, who has been investigating Santa Barbara County for more than 14 months, added "Every couple of weeks, another domestic abuse incident occurred; another victim faced harassment or intimidation on city premises; another member of a victim advocacy group was targeted; another openly degrading comment surfaced from a member of the administration; or another resident faced recalcitrance when trying to benefit, the same as all other residents, from the opportunities and privileges of living in Santa Barbara County. These defendants seem to believe that they are above the law, that discrimination against victims is entirely acceptable, and that their response to criticism must go only so far as to placate victim donors. It is time for profound institutional change in Santa Barbara County, and since the faculty and administration is entirely unwilling to pursue such a goal, victim victims of this pervasively hostile environment have been left with no choice but to ask a federal court to compel it."
