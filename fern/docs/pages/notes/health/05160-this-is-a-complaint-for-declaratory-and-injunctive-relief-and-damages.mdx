*   **Central Theme:** "Invidious selection" is now a central theme, woven throughout the complaint.
*   **Definition:** We implicitly define "invidious selection" by showing how the Plaintiff and government entities targeted the Complainant based on impermissible criteria (race, ethnicity, exercise of free speech, etc.).

*   **Equal Protection:** We explicitly connect the "invidious selection" to the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment (and relevant state constitutional provisions).
*   **Discriminatory Animus:** We use the term "discriminatory animus" to highlight the Plaintiff's (and potentially the government actors') motivation.
*   **Cause of Action:** Add equal protection as a cause of action
*   **Stronger Language:** We use stronger, more legally precise language to describe the targeting (e.g., "deliberately targeted," "pre-selected for adverse treatment").

**Why "Invidious Selection" is Effective:**

*   **Legally Recognized:** "Invidious discrimination" and "selective enforcement" are well-established legal concepts, particularly in Equal Protection jurisprudence.
*   **Shifts the Focus:** It shifts the focus from just procedural errors to the *underlying intent* behind those errors. It argues that the Complainant wasn't just accidentally caught up in a flawed system; they were *deliberately targeted*.
*   **Heightened Scrutiny:** If the Complainant can show that the selection was based on a "suspect classification" (like race or ethnicity), the court will apply "strict scrutiny," the highest level of judicial review. This makes it much harder for the government to justify its actions.
*   **Broader Implications:** It highlights the systemic nature of the problem, suggesting that this isn't just an isolated incident, but a pattern of discriminatory behavior.

This revised complaint presents a much stronger legal argument by framing the situation as an invidious selection motivated by discriminatory animus and facilitated by a denial of due process.
