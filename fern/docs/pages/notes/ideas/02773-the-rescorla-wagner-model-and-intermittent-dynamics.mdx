## 1. Demand Characteristics: The Subject Responds to the Frame, Not the Facts

**Commonwealth v. DiGiambattista**, 442 Mass. 423, 813 N.E.2d 516 (Mass. 2004). [Commonwealth v. DiGiambattista](https://www.cetient.com/case/commonwealth-v-digiambattista-6578995)

Police told the suspect they had a videotape of the crime (they did not) and then repeatedly minimized the offense, calling it �understandable, justifiable, and not particularly serious,� while implying counseling and leniency would follow a confession. The suspect confessed to a story that matched the police narrative but directly contradicted forensic facts. The court found:

\> �A person being asked by an interrogator to confess to a crime that is repeatedly described as understandable, justifiable, and not particularly serious would likely assume that giving the requested confession will result in lenient treatment.�

The suspect was not responding to the crime. He was responding to the interrogation frame. The court held that when police combine trickery with minimization, voluntariness is compromised because the suspect is complying with the perceived demand, not the underlying truth.

**People v. Kogut**, 806 NYS2d 366 (N.Y. Sup. Ct. 2005). [People v. Kogut](https://www.cetient.com/case/people-v-kogut-6306608)

The court reviewed Dr. Saul Kassin�s experimental research demonstrating that subjects confessed to hypothetical crimes they never committed �in order to gain a minor reward or avoid a minimal punishment.� The court accepted this as scientifically valid: the subjects detected the experimental demand and complied with it. The interrogator�s data were therefore measuring compliance, not guilt.

**United States v. Bundy**, 966 F. Supp. 2d 1180 (D.N.M. 2013). [United States v. Bundy](https://www.cetient.com/case/united-states-v-bundy-8727700)

The interrogator used incomplete facts to pressure the suspect. The suspect eventually �confessed� to matters �as to which she had no actual recollection,� instead reasoning by inference from the incomplete facts the agent provided. The court found:

\> �The defendant appears to this Court to have been reasoning by inference from the incomplete facts provided by Agent Sullivan rather than actually recalling the accident.�

This is demand characteristics in practice: the subject was responding to the interrogator�s supplied framework, not to her own memory.
