	I, the Petitioner, move to obtain a temporary restraining order without notice under California Code of Civil Procedure Section 527 and California Family Code Section 6300. California Family Code Section 6300 authorizes the court to grant a temporary restraining order to prevent domestic violence and provide the protected party with a safe environment. (Cal. Fam. Code � 6300; see also Nakamura v. Parker (2007) 156 Cal.App.4th 327, 335.)  California Code of Civil Procedure Section 527 governs the issuance of a temporary restraining order. Under that Section, a court may issue a temporary restraining order without notice to the non-moving party only if:

(a) It appears from the facts shown by affidavit that great or irreparable harm would otherwise be suffered by any party to the action. (Cal. Code Civ. Proc. � 527(c).) "The court must be cautious in exercising its discretion to grant ex parte relief, as this procedure can result in an order being issued without the opposing party having an opportunity to be heard." (In re Marriage of Ramirez (2011) 198 Cal.App.4th 336, 344.) California Rules of Court further require that, in support of an ex parte application, the movant must submit a declaration stating:

(1) that within a reasonable time before the motion the moving party informed the opposing party or the opposing party's attorney when and where the motion would be made; or (2) that the moving party in good faith attempted to inform the opposing party and the opposing party's attorney but was unable to do so, specifying the efforts made to inform them; or (3) that for reasons specified the moving party should not be required to inform the opposing party's attorney. (Cal. Rules of Court, rule 3.1204.)

A temporary restraining order generally is considered "an extraordinary remedy that may only be awarded upon a clear showing that the plaintiff is entitled to such relief." (In re Marriage of Nadkarni (2009) 173 Cal.App.4th 1483, 1495.) The moving party has the burden of persuasion. (Harris v. Stampolis (2016) 248 Cal.App.4th 484, 499.) To obtain a temporary restraining order, the movant must show: (1) a likelihood of success on the merits; (2) a likelihood of irreparable harm to the movant in the absence of preliminary relief; (3) that the balance of equities tips in favor of the moving party; and (4) that an injunction is in the public interest.
