So here's a question: Who should get isolated?

The person who's being harmed, or the person doing the harming?

Because I see this pattern constantly.

The person who speaks up gets managed

and the person causing the harm gets accommodated.

So here are three truths that help clear this up:

Number one, we isolate the wrong person.

The one who reacts gets labelled �dramatic,�

�difficult,� or �too much,� while the one who caused the harm stays comfortable.

That's backwards. If someone's reaction is the problem,

start by looking at what provoked it.

Number two, �be the bigger person� gets weaponized.

It's supposed to mean: don't become petty,

but it often gets used to mean: tolerate disrespect and mistreatment

so everyone else can stay comfortable.

And number three, the real question isn't who's calmer,

it's who's accountable. Who owns what they did?

Who changes the behaviour?

Who respects a boundary without socially injuring you?

Because when a group protects the wrong person,

it teaches everyone exactly what gets rewarded.

And that's how dysfunctional families develop and toxic cultures thrive.

Watch who gets protected and what gets rewarded.

And follow to guess less and lead more.
