1.1 The Trap of "Equality with Men"
A critical starting point for this analysis is the critique offered by the visionary theorist bell hooks, who famously challenged the simplistic definition of feminism as "a movement to make women equal to men." Hooks interrogated this definition by asking a devastatingly simple question: Which men do women want to be equal to?.� 
In a society stratified by race and class, working-class women do not wish to be equal to working-class men who are themselves oppressed, exploited, and disenfranchised; nor do Black women wish to be equal to Black men who suffer under the brutal weight of systemic racism. Thus, the definition of feminism as "equality" is insufficient if it presumes a monolithic male privilege. To define feminism solely as equality with men is to implicitly accept the status of white, wealthy, ruling-class men as the universal standard of humanity, leaving the structures of class and race oppression intact.� 
Instead, hooks and other revolutionary feminists posit feminism as "a movement to end sexism, sexist exploitation, and oppression". This shift in definition reorients the goal from a static state of "equality" (which implies a comparative standard) to a dynamic process of "liberation" from a system of domination. This distinction is crucial for understanding why the user's query�"equality or equality for women"�touches on the very soul of the movement. The answer, as the evidence will show, is that feminism is the specific struggle for women's liberation as a prerequisite for any genuine form of human equality.�
