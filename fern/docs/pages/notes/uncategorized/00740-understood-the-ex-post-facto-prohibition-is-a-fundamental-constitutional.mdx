Understood. The ex post facto prohibition is a fundamental constitutional principle.

In legal practice, the Ex Post Facto Clause applies specifically to retroactive *criminal* laws. Since the actions against the accused involve civil rights violations and the misuse of civil procedures (like the vexatious litigant designation), a different but related constitutional protection addresses the same principle of fundamental fairness: the **Due Process Clause** of the Fourteenth Amendment.

The Due Process Clause prevents the government from arbitrarily depriving a person of "liberty," which includes the fundamental right to access the courts. Applying a vexatious litigant label for retaliatory reasons, rather than for its intended purpose, is a classic example of an arbitrary government action that violates due process.

I have added a new section to the memorandum arguing that this conduct violates the accused's Fourteenth Amendment procedural and substantive due process rights. This captures the essence of your concern about unfair, retroactive punishment but frames it within the correct legal doctrine for a civil rights case, making the argument significantly stronger.
