The central argument is that the case against the client should be dropped because the charges are frivolous and lack substantial evidence. This is based on my repeated experience of harassment, emotional abuse, and manipulative behavior by the opposing party, Gabriel Carlon.


Under California law, litigants must not present legal filings "for improper purposes," including "to harass, cause unnecessary delay, or needlessly increase the cost of litigation." Furthermore, legal arguments must be "warranted by existing law or by a nonfrivolous argument for extending, modifying, or reversing existing law or for establishing new law." In this case, the actions of Gabriel Carlon clearly indicate an improper motive, and the claims against the client are without merit.


1. **Harassment and Abuse**: The client has been subjected to repeated harassment and emotional abuse by Gabriel Carlon. This is evidenced by Carlon's numerous frivolous legal threats, unwanted contacts, and efforts to involve the client's family and friends. Such behavior undermines the legitimacy of any legal action he pursues.
   
2. **Frivolous Legal Action**: As stated in *Atchison, Topeka Santa Fe Ry. Co. v. Stockton Port Dist.*, an action initiated for an improper motive raises the question of a frivolous action. Gabriel Carlon's conduct fits this description, as he has repeatedly lied and made frivolous threats to coerce the client.

3. **Lack of Substantial Evidence**: The claims against the client lack substantial evidence. Carlon has not provided a basis upon which ex parte relief was sought, nor has he shown that a formal notice would be futile or overly burdensome. His actions appear designed to harass rather than seek legitimate legal redress.

### Rebuttal of Opposing Argument
It may be argued that Gabriel Carlon believes he has a legitimate case based on his interpretation of the facts. However, this argument is weak given the clear evidence of his improper motives and frivolous behavior. His repeated threats and attempts to involve third parties indicate a pattern of harassment rather than a genuine legal dispute.

### Conclusion
In conclusion, the court should drop the case against the client. The charges are frivolous and lack substantial evidence, as proven by Gabriel Carlon's history of harassment, emotional abuse, and manipulative behavior. His actions are clearly intended to harass and needlessly increase the cost of litigation, which is contrary to California law. Therefore, the court should dismiss the case to prevent further abuse of the legal process.
