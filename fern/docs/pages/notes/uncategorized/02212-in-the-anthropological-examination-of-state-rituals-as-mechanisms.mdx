The U.S. Supreme Court upheld a Pennsylvania law requiring public school students to stand, salute the flag, and recite the Pledge of Allegiance, with noncompliance resulting in expulsion.

From an anthropological perspective, the mandatory pledge functions as a state-sanctioned ritual designed to reinforce dominant cultural narratives and values�manifestations of cultural hegemony. Such rituals are not neutral; they serve to naturalize and perpetuate specific ideologies, embedding them within the daily practices of citizens. The punitive response to dissent, exemplified by the expulsion of the Gobitis children, underscores how state rituals operate not merely as expressions of national unity but as instruments for enforcing conformity and marginalizing divergent worldviews.

This case highlights the role of state rituals in shaping collective consciousness, exerting subtle yet profound influence over the cultural landscape by delineating the boundaries of acceptable belief and behavior.
