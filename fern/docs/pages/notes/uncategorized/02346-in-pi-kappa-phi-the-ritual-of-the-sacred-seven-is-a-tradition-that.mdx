In Pi Kappa Phi, the ritual of the "Sacred Seven" is a tradition that has been practiced for over a century. The ritual is not meant to be cruel or malicious, but rather an opportunity for members to demonstrate their brotherhood and unity as they collectively face and overcome a challenge together. The seven men chosen are randomly selected from among the Fraternity's members by the Grand Chapter (the governing body of Pi Kappa Phi). These seven men are given no prior knowledge about the ritual, and only one of them is chosen to be "sacrificed" - that is, to suffer a public humiliation. The rest of the group stand with their backs towards the altar, unaware who will be sacrificed until it's time for the ritual. The initiation ceremony begins when all seven men are called forward by the Grand Chapter officers and asked to step onto the altar. Afterward, one man is chosen at random, typically using a method such as drawing straws or flipping a coin. This man is then blindfolded and led away from the altar while the others watch silently. The rest of the group is instructed to remain on the altar for an hour without speaking or moving until they receive word about their brother's fate. Afterward, they are allowed to leave, but only after returning to their seats and remaining silent for another hour before being dismissed. While this ritual may seem harsh at first glance, it serves a purpose within Pi Kappa Phi by promoting unity, trust, and resilience among its members. It also emphasizes the importance of selflessness and sacrifice in brotherhood.
