## 1. **Geographic Expansion Patterns**
### **Early Regional Focus (1904�1940s)**
- **Southern Roots:** The fraternity began in the South (College of Charleston, SC) and initially expanded to nearby states (Georgia, North Carolina, Alabama, Tennessee, Florida).
- **First Long-Distance Chapter:** Gamma at the University of California, Berkeley (1909) marked the fraternity�s first major expansion outside the South, signaling a shift toward national growth.
- **Slow Nationalization:** By 1930, most chapters were still in the South, with only a few in the Midwest and West.

### **Post-WWII National Growth (1950s�1970s)**
- **Rapid Expansion:** The 1950s�1970s saw a surge in new chapters, especially in the Midwest (Ohio, Illinois, Indiana, Michigan) and West (California, Oregon, Washington).
- **Clustered Growth:** Chapters were often founded in states with existing Pi Kappa Phi presence, suggesting a "hub-and-spoke" model of expansion.

### **Modern Era (1980s�Present)**
- **Northeast and West Coast:** Increased presence in the Northeast (Pennsylvania, New York, Maine) and West Coast (California, Oregon, Washington).
- **Sun Belt Growth:** Significant expansion in Florida, Texas, and Arizona, reflecting broader demographic and higher education trends.

## 2. **Temporal Patterns: Waves of Expansion**
### **Founding Waves**
- **1904�1930:** 36 chapters, mostly in the South.
- **1950s�1960s:** 50+ new chapters, including first chapters in the Midwest and West.
- **1970s�1990s:** Over 100 new chapters, with a focus on recolonization and filling gaps in the national map.
- **2000s�Present:** Slower but steady growth, with an emphasis on rechartering closed chapters and strategic expansion.

### **Periods of Stagnation**
- **Great Depression (1930s) and WWII (1940s):** Minimal growth due to economic and social upheaval.
- **1970s Oil Crisis and Campus Unrest:** Some chapters closed or struggled, but the fraternity rebounded in the 1980s.

## 3. **Chapter Longevity and Stability**
### **Continuously Active Chapters**
- **Oldest Active Chapters:** Alpha (College of Charleston), Iota (Georgia Tech), Omicron (University of Alabama), and Lambda (University of Georgia) have been active for over a century, with few or no interruptions.
- **Stable Regions:** Southern chapters (e.g., Alabama, Georgia, Tennessee) tend to have longer tenures and fewer closures.

### **Volatile Chapters**
- **Frequent Closures/Reopenings:** Chapters in the Northeast (e.g., Cornell, Penn State, Rensselaer) and some Midwestern schools (e.g., Illinois, Ohio State) have experienced multiple closures and reopenings, often due to disciplinary issues or university policies.
- **West Coast Challenges:** Chapters in California and Oregon have faced more closures, possibly due to stricter university regulations or cultural differences.

### **Permanent Closures**
- **Disciplinary Actions:** Chapters closed permanently are often linked to hazing, alcohol violations, or failure to meet fraternity standards (e.g., Beta at Presbyterian College, Epsilon at Davidson).
- **University Bans:** Some closures resulted from university-wide fraternity bans or moratoriums.

## 4. **Disciplinary and Rechartering Trends**
### **Common Reasons for Closure**
- **Hazing and Alcohol Violations:** The most frequent cause of chapter closures, especially in the 1980s�2000s.
- **Low Membership or Financial Issues:** Smaller chapters or those at smaller schools are more vulnerable.
- **University Policy Changes:** Some closures followed university decisions to ban or restrict fraternities.

### **Rechartering Success**
- **Recolonization Efforts:** Pi Kappa Phi has a strong track record of recolonizing closed chapters, often within 5�10 years (e.g., Upsilon at Illinois, Alpha Sigma at Tennessee).
- **Associate Chapter Model:** The fraternity uses "associate chapters" or colonies to rebuild closed chapters, ensuring smoother transitions back to active status.
