### **III. LEGAL STANDARD**

It is well-settled that a party cannot create a genuine issue of material fact by submitting an affidavit that contradicts the affiant's own prior sworn deposition testimony. This is known as the "sham affidavit doctrine." The rule exists to "prevent a party who has been examined at length on deposition from raising an issue of fact simply by submitting an affidavit contradicting his own prior testimony." *See, e.g., Seigler v. Wal-Mart Stores TX*, 30 F.4th 472 (5th Cir. 2022).

The sham affidavit doctrine applies not only to direct, affirmative contradictions but also to a "sham by omission." This occurs when a witness is questioned about a topic during deposition and omits certain facts, only to later raise them for the first time in an affidavit to defeat summary judgment. When the deposition questioning was sufficient to elicit the information, a subsequent, contradictory affidavit is considered a sham. The purpose of this rule is to preserve the integrity of the litigation process and prevent a party from unfairly "ambushing" the opposition with new testimony that should have been disclosed during discovery.
