Litigiants should be able to complain about what they believe to be unlawful practices without fear of retaliation. It also makes no sense to extend protection to someone, like plaintiff, who did not make any indication, did not engage in any meaningful communication, did not seek any informal dispute resolution, and did nothing to take voluntary steps to avoid or remedy a perceived violation. Although section _____ language requires the person seeking its protection to oppose �any practices forbidden under� the DVPA�which seems to require that the practices actually be forbidden�courts have expanded the statute beyond its language to permit a retaan employee �who has complained of or opposed conduct that the employee reasonably believes to be discriminatory, even when a court later determines the conduct was not actually prohibited by the FEHA.� (Maj. opn., ante, 32 Cal.Rptr.3d at p. 445, 116 P.3d at p. 1131, citing Miller, supra, 36 Cal.4th at p. 473, 30 Cal.Rptr.3d at 820, 115 P.3d at 95�96.) I agree with this expansion and the policy behind it. But if we are to interpret the statute as not requiring conduct that was actually illegal but merely conduct the individual believes to be illegal, then surely we must require that the plaintiff communicate this belief to the other party. It makes no sense to hold both that the conduct need not be unlawful and that the plaintiff need not complain of it.
