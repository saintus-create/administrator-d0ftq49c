This case is highly relevant as it directly addresses a scenario where a defendant (an insurance agent) exploited a victim's disability (post-poliosclerosis) and position of trust to financially deceive them. The court explicitly found that the defendant's actions constituted financial exploitation because the victim's impairment rendered her 'incapable of avoiding or preventing' the offense. Supporting paragraph: 'Because of her physical disability and the position of confidence and trust which the defendant occupied with her, it is clear that Ms. Smith was unable to avoid financial exploitation by the defendant.' This matters because it establishes a legal precedent that a perpetrator's knowledge of a victim's disability, combined with a fiduciary relationship, can satisfy the elements of exploiting a disabled person's vulnerability.
