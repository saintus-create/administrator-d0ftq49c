What Preponderance of the Evidence Means
Preponderance of the evidence is defined as "proof which produces a reasonable belief of the probability of the existence of the material facts." [Hart v. Thereault] More specifically, it requires showing that a claim is "more probably true than not true" � essentially a greater than 50% probability. [preponderance of the evidence | Wex - Cornell Law School] As one court explained, this means "the weight, credit, and value of the aggregate evidence on either side," with the evidence being more convincing and worthy of belief than that offered in opposition. [Burden of Proof and Burden of Evidence (RULE 131)]
General Application
In most civil hearings, the preponderance standard applies to both parties. [Key Takeaways] Typically, the plaintiff bears the initial burden of proving their claims by preponderance, [Hawkins v. Davie] and if the respondent raises affirmative defenses, they must generally prove those defenses by the same preponderance standard.
Exceptions and Higher Standards
However, certain defenses or claims may require a higher standard. For instance, some proceedings require "clear and convincing evidence" � a standard requiring proof that creates a firm belief or conviction in the truth of the allegation, which is more demanding than preponderance.
