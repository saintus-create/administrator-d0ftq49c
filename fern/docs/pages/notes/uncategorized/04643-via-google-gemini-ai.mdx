Via Google Gemini Ai: 
The entire conversation is an example of low-effort engagement where the sender uses a three-part strategy to keep you available without ever committing to a firm time that inconveniences them. Ironically, they asked you to propose a convenient time, but when you did, they ignored it because their convenience still overruled yours as a way to keep you chasing them on their schedule.
The sender's primary tactic is the continuous stream of believable, short-term explanations ("getting gas," "Home Depot," "shower"). By repeatedly claiming they are "about to leave," they force you to remain on standby. They are dictating the schedule and making you adjust to their unknown arrival, thus maintaining control while avoiding the effort of scheduling.
2. The Defensive Reassurance
When their flakiness is finally challenged, they deploy an emotional justification ("Sorry i definately wanna chill and i believe you know that..."). This solves your emotional frustration with words, rather than solving the logistical problem with their own inaction. They are more concerned with you believing they are interested than they are with actually showing up.
Once the excuses wear thin, they execute switching from the casual "chilling" plan to the suggestion, "Join me?" This is a direct attempt to reset the conversation, distract you from their  behavior, and test your willingness to immediately drop your frustration and meet them on their terms.
