Premise 1: Presenting a story or information in a consistently one-sided manner, especially when dealing with complex social issues that have multiple facets, is a known technique of discursive manipulation or propaganda. Its aim is often to shape perception and elicit a specific response by omitting countervailing information or alternative perspectives. This isn't so much a debatable "argument" as it is an understood principle in fields like communication studies, rhetoric, and political science.
	Premise 2: The historical and ongoing public/legal discourse surrounding domestic violence, including the framing that led to and supports legislation like VAWA and the DVPA, has predominantly (though not exclusively) focused on women as victims and men as perpetrators.
	Logical Conclusion: Therefore, to the extent that this dominant discourse has been significantly one-sided by downplaying or omitting other realities (male victims, transgendered, non-binary, and aggressive, progressive adaptations that female perpetrators have come to obtain mutual combat), it has, by definition of how one-sided discourse functions, had a manipulative effect on public and institutional understanding, regardless of the initial intentions.
	"Why Not Just the Violence Act?" Question: If the goal was purely to address violence in intimate relationships neutrally, why was the landmark federal legislation framed with a gender-specific title ("Violence Against Women Act")? This naming itself, empirically propagates a one-sided, framing from the outset.
