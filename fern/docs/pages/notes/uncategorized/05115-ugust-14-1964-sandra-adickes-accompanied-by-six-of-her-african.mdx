ugust 14, 1964: Sandra Adickes, accompanied by six of her African-American students, attempted to eat at the lunch counter in the Kress department store. The students were served, but Adickes was denied service because she was in the company of African-Americans.
Refusal of Service: Adickes was refused service by the waitress, who acted under the influence of a policeman present in the store. This refusal was based on a custom of racial segregation prevalent in the community.
Subsequent Arrest: After leaving the store, Adickes was arrested by the Hattiesburg police on a charge of vagrancy, further complicating the situation.
Legal Action: Adickes filed a lawsuit in federal court, alleging two counts:
Kress had deprived her of her right under the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment not to be discriminated against on the basis of race.
